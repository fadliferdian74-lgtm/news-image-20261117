# Sources — "The World Today", Tuesday 17 November 2026

Every story on the front page was reported on Tuesday 17 November 2026 and is
confirmed by at least two independent credible outlets (wire services and
major media). Facts and figures below are exactly as reported; the page's
labels match them. Claims that a source itself flags as unconfirmed are
labelled as claims on the page, or left off it.

## Lead story

1. **Ukraine strikes the Kerch Bridge (Crimea Bridge)**
   - Sources: Reuters (Nov 17, via 935thelloyd.com / SRN syndication); Associated
     Press (Nov 17, via mtpr.org); Bloomberg (Nov 17, via military.com).
   - Facts: Ukrainian drones and missiles struck the bridge linking southern
     Russia to Crimea for a second straight day. Ukraine's General Staff said
     an arch and a span of the road section collapsed into the Kerch Strait,
     severing the road link; the rail line was damaged but trains resumed.
     Crimea's Russian-installed governor Sergei Aksyonov said two people were
     killed and traffic was suspended. President Zelenskyy called it an
     "unprecedented" seven-stage operation and retaliation for Russian strikes
     on Ukrainian infrastructure. Russia said its air defences downed 1,441
     Ukrainian drones overnight; Russian strikes damaged bridges in Kyiv.

## Main stories

2. **Trump opens Asia trip with Quad leaders in Manila**
   - Sources: Reuters (Nov 17, via srnnews.com); Associated Press reporting
     (Nov 17, via worldpulsewire.com); BusinessWorld (Nov 17).
   - Facts: US President Donald Trump met the leaders of India, Japan and
     Australia in Manila — the Quad's first leaders' meeting in two years — at
     the start of a three-day Asia trip. Ahead: the APEC summit in Shenzhen and
     a first-ever trilateral meeting with China's Xi Jinping and Russia's
     Vladimir Putin on Thursday (Nov 19).

3. **Bahrain intercepts Iranian drones; US strikes Qeshm Island**
   - Sources: Reuters (Nov 17); AFP (Nov 17, explosions heard in Manama);
     The Guardian live coverage (Nov 17).
   - Facts: Bahrain said its air defences intercepted Iranian air attacks — the
     third Iranian drone attack on the kingdom in five days. Iran's
     Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted a US military radar system at
     Sheikh Isa Air Base (a claim; no hit independently confirmed). No
     casualties or damage were reported. The US later struck an Iranian missile
     launch site on Qeshm Island (US official to Reuters). Saudi Arabia, Qatar,
     Jordan and Egypt condemned the attack on Bahrain.

4. **Nvidia posts record quarter amid CEO turmoil**
   - Sources: Reuters (Trading Day, Nov 17); Wall Street Journal (Nov 17, via
     NY Post and syndication); CNBC (via NY Post).
   - Facts: Nvidia reported record third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up
     103% year on year and above the ~$109.1 billion expected, and forecast
     fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion. Shares rose about 4% in
     after-hours trading. The WSJ reported the SEC has opened a civil
     investigation into Nvidia's disclosures. Former CEO Jensen Huang, who
     resigned on Nov 12, said he would be open to returning; board chair Harvey
     Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate him. CFO Colette Kress
     is interim CEO. Markets on Tuesday: S&P 500 −1.0%, Dow −1.6%, Nasdaq
     −0.7%, Europe and UK −1.8% (worst day since March), Japan −1.3% (Reuters).

5. **United States loses measles elimination status**
   - Sources: Pan American Health Organization announcement (Nov 17) and CDC
     joint statement (Nov 17); BBC (Nov 17); Science News (Nov 17).
   - Facts: PAHO declared on Nov 17 that measles transmission has been
     re-established in the United States, which loses the elimination status it
     held since 2000; Mexico and Bolivia also lost the status. The CDC had
     confirmed 5,412 US cases in 2026 as of Nov 12 — the most in a year since
     1991 — with about 96% in people unvaccinated or of unknown status.

## Briefs

6. **Sudan: drones behind most verified civilian deaths — UN**
   - Sources: Reuters (Emma Farge, Geneva, Nov 17); UN OHCHR statement.
   - Facts: The UN human rights office confirmed 2,230 civilian deaths in
     Sudan's civil war from January to October 2026; over 1,630 — nearly
     three-quarters — were killed by drones. High Commissioner Volker Türk
     condemned a "brazen disregard for the laws of war."

7. **Gas tanker sinks off Bulgaria; all 19 crew rescued**
   - Sources: Associated Press (Nov 17); Reuters (Nov 17); BTA (Bulgarian
     News Agency).
   - Facts: The Liberian-flagged LPG tanker Gas Husky sank in the Black Sea
     about 50 nautical miles east of Byala, Bulgaria, on Tuesday. All 19
     Filipino crew were rescued by Bulgarian helicopter to Varna. It was the
     second ship to sink in the area in two days (the cargo ship Betul 8 went
     down on Monday; its 12 crew were also rescued). The cause was not
     officially confirmed; the captain reported drone strikes.

8. **Japan scrambles jets over suspected Russian airspace breach**
   - Sources: Reuters (Nov 17); Kyodo News (Nov 17); Japan Defense Ministry.
   - Facts: Two aircraft believed to be Russian entered Japanese airspace
     northwest of Okinoshima Island, Fukuoka, for about three minutes each
     shortly after 4 a.m. — the first confirmed violation since September
     2024. Japan scrambled fighters and lodged a "very stern protest."

9. **8,000+ hoax bomb threats rattle Europe — Reuters investigation**
   - Sources: Reuters exclusive investigation (Nov 17); Reuters podcast
     (Nov 17).
   - Facts: A Reuters tally found more than 8,000 hoax bomb threats sent to
     schools, airports and businesses across Europe over the past month, most
     targeting the UK, Ireland and Italy (about 6,300 Italian schools on
     Nov 11 alone). Investigators suspect the messages are linked and officials
     suspect possible foreign interference; Europol is investigating.

10. **Houthis advance on Aden; Saudi Arabia replaces commander**
    - Sources: Reuters (Nov 15–17); AFP (Nov 17, via France 24 / Times of
      Israel briefing); Associated Press (Nov 17).
    - Facts: Houthi forces advanced along Yemen's southern coast toward Aden,
      the interim capital, after government forces withdrew from the Bab
      el-Mandeb area to defend the city. Saudi Arabia replaced the commander
      of its coalition joint forces (royal decree, Nov 16).

11. **Mongolia's president says he will resign**
    - Sources: Associated Press (Nov 17); Reuters (Nov 17); Montsame.
    - Facts: President Ukhnaa Khurelsukh announced on Tuesday he will resign
      after accusations — which he denies — that he received $25 million in
      connection with a 2019 railway contract awarded when he was prime
      minister. Parliament must accept the resignation.

12. **Turkey takes opposition TV channel Sozcu off air**
    - Sources: Reuters (Tuvan Gumrukcu, Ankara, Nov 17).
    - Facts: Turkish authorities took pro-opposition Sozcu TV off air in a
      pre-dawn raid on Tuesday, appointed a state trustee (TMSF) to run the
      channel and website, and detained four executives — the sixth major news
      outlet given a trustee since 2022.

13. **Canada and China expand tariff relief**
    - Sources: Reuters (Shenzhen, Nov 17); Xinhua (Nov 17).
    - Facts: Meeting Xi Jinping in Shenzhen ahead of APEC, Canada's PM Mark
      Carney agreed a deal under which China will cut tariffs on about
      C$400 million ($280 million) of Canadian exports including pork and
      seafood, while Canada extends tariff relief on some Chinese steel and
      aluminium products by two years.

14. **US tightens curbs on China's AI chip access**
    - Sources: Reuters exclusive (Nov 17); Reuters podcast (Nov 17).
    - Facts: The Trump administration advanced new restrictions on Chinese
      access to advanced AI chips and overseas data-center equipment, despite
      lobbying by Amazon, Google and Meta. China accused the US of "economic
      coercion."

15. **ONGC gas leak in Andhra Pradesh brought under control**
    - Sources: Reuters (Nov 17, via The Straits Times); ANI / The Hindu
      BusinessLine (Nov 17).
    - Facts: A gas leak began at about 5 a.m. during workover operations at
      ONGC's Tatipaka well in Geddada village, Konaseema district; about 800
      residents were evacuated. The flow was controlled at about 3 p.m. after a
      10-hour operation. No injuries or casualties were reported.

16. **Dutch coalition survives crisis talks**
    - Sources: Reuters (Amsterdam, Nov 17); Dutch News (Nov 17).
    - Facts: Prime Minister Rob Jetten said the minority coalition (D66, CDA,
      VVD) had found a way to keep governing after four days of crisis talks
      triggered by a parliamentary wealth-tax vote.

17. **Nuclear regulators warn over Ukraine grid attacks**
    - Sources: Reuters (Nov 16–17); IAEA statement (Nov 14).
    - Facts: Nuclear safety regulators from 38 countries (HERCA, WENRA, NORA)
      warned the risk of a nuclear accident in Ukraine will be highest this
      winter as Russian strikes strain the power grid that cools its reactors,
      and urged Russia to stop attacks on energy infrastructure.

18. **Sport: Haaland fires Norway to first World Cup since 1998**
    - Sources: AFP (via Apex Football); All Football roundup (Nov 17).
    - Facts: Erling Haaland scored twice as Norway beat Italy 4–1 at San Siro
      to qualify for their first World Cup since 1998; he finished qualifying
      with 16 goals. Italy must go through the play-offs.
